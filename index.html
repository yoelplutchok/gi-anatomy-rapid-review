1Building the gut tube

  • Head fold → foregut. Tail fold → hindgut + cloaca. Middle stays open to the yolk sac by the vitelline duct → midgut.
  • Lateral body folds close the tube and the front body wall. Fail to fuse → gastroschisis.
  • Ectoderm on endoderm, no mesoderm: stomodeum (mouth) and proctodeum (anus).

2Foregut: esophagus to duodenum

Plug and reopen

  • Tracheoesophageal septum: trachea in front, esophagus behind.
  • Lining overgrows into a near-solid rod, then reopens by apoptosis (recanalization) by wk 8. Same in the duodenum.
  • None = atresia (no lumen, epithelial plug). Partial = stenosis (narrow).

Esophageal atresia ± TEF

  • Newborn drools, chokes and turns blue at the first feed. Mother had polyhydramnios.
  • Most common (~85%): blind upper pouch + lower esophagus joined to trachea.
  • Air in the stomach only if the lower esophagus meets the trachea. Pure atresia → gasless belly.

Amniotic fluid

  • Fetus swallows it; kidneys make it (urine).
  • Polyhydramnios = can't swallow: esophageal or duodenal atresia. Low blocks (anus, colon) don't cause it.
  • Oligohydramnios = no urine: bilateral renal agenesis.

Stomach rotation

  1. Dorsal wall grows faster → greater curvature.
  2. Stomach rotates 90° to the left (clockwise from above).
  3. Dorsal mesogastrium swings left and hangs as the greater omentum (4 layers). Space behind the stomach = omental bursa (lesser sac).
  4. Duodenum swings into a C on the right, presses on the back wall → secondary retro.

Spleen: wk 5, mesoderm between the 2 layers of the dorsal mesogastrium, behind the stomach. Rotation carries it left. Fetal blood-forming organ.

Vomiting newborn

Bile enters at the major papilla. Block above → non-bilious. Below → bilious.

3Liver and pancreas

  • Hepatic diverticulum buds from the ventral lowest foregut into the septum transversum.
  • Cranial part → hepatocytes, bile capillaries. Caudal (cystic) part → gallbladder + cystic duct. Stalk → bile duct.
  • Liver cells = endoderm. Fibrous tissue + Kupffer cells = mesoderm (septum transversum).
  • Liver splits the ventral mesentery: liver–stomach → lesser omentum (portal triad in free edge); liver–front wall → falciform lig. Umbilical vein → ligamentum teres; ductus venosus → ligamentum venosum.
Dorsal budVentral bud
StartsDorsal duodenumVentral, with the liver bud
MakesRest of head, neck, body, tailUncinate process + lower head
  • Duodenal rotation carries the ventral bud behind → posterior and inferior to the dorsal bud, bringing the bile duct. Buds and ducts fuse.
  • Annular pancreas: bilobed ventral bud, one lobe each side → ring around 2nd part → blocks it.
  • Pancreas divisum: ducts never fuse.

4Midgut rotation

U-shaped primary intestinal loop; the SMA is its axis; apex tied to yolk sac by the vitelline duct.

  • Cranial arm → small intestine (grows fast, coils).
  • Caudal arm → lower ileum, cecum, appendix, ascending + proximal transverse colon.
  1. Wk 6: too little room → herniates into the umbilical cord (normal, physiologic); turns 90° CCW.
  2. Wk 10: returns, turns 180° more → total 270° CCW. Small bowel first, cecum last. Appendix = a pouch off the cecum.
  3. Ascending + descending colon fuse to the back wall (secondary retro). Cecum, appendix, sigmoid keep mesenteries.

Normal end: cecum in right lower quadrant; transverse colon in front of the SMA.

ErrorFinding
Nonrotation (only 90°)Small bowel right, colon left; cecum midline
Reversed (90° clockwise)Transverse colon behind the SMA

Meckel diverticulum = leftover gut end of the vitelline duct, on the ileum. Rule of 2s: ~2%, 2 in long, 2 ft from the ileocecal junction.

  • Stomach tissue → ulcer → painless rectal bleeding in a child.
  • Whole duct open (vitelline fistula) → stool from the umbilicus.
  • Fibrous cord to umbilicus → bowel twists.

7Testis descent

  • Testis forms high on the back wall → testicular artery from the aorta; lymph → para-aortic nodes.
  • Gubernaculum guides it down; processus vaginalis (peritoneum) leads. The tunnel = inguinal canal. Female gubernaculum → ovarian lig + round lig of uterus.
  • Processus stays open → congenital indirect hernia; fluid trapped → hydrocele. Testis stuck on its path → cryptorchidism (↑ cancer).

Cremasteric reflex (L1–L2): stroke upper inner thigh → ilioinguinal (sensory) → genital branch of genitofemoral (motor) → testis rises. Lost if the genital branch is cut.

7Testis and epididymis

  • Testis forms high on the back wall → testicular artery from the aorta (below renal arteries).
  • Veins: pampiniform plexus → one testicular vein at the deep ring. Right → IVC. Left → left renal vein (right angle).
  • Varicocele: dilated pampiniform veins, "bag of worms", left side (right-angle entry; nutcracker: left renal vein squeezed between SMA and aorta).
  • Cooling: pampiniform veins take heat from the testicular artery (countercurrent exchange); cremaster and dartos help.
  • Seminiferous tubules → rete testis → efferent ductules → head of epididymis.
  • Epididymis (on the back of the testis): stores + matures sperm. Ductus deferens starts at its tail → up the cord → deep ring → pelvis → joins seminal gland duct = ejaculatory duct. Vasectomy: cut in the scrotum, both sides.

1Peritoneum

  • Parietal: lines the wall; somatic nerves → sharp, well-localized pain; from somatic lateral plate mesoderm.
  • Visceral: covers organs; no somatic sensory nerves → vague, poorly localized pain; from splanchnic mesoderm. Endoderm and ectoderm make no peritoneum.
  • Peritoneal cavity = potential space with only a film of serous fluid. No organs, no lymph nodes inside. Closed in males; open in females (tubes, uterus, vagina).
  • Intraperitoneal: covered almost all round, on a mesentery, movable (still not "inside" the cavity: fist pushed into a balloon).
  • Retroperitoneal: behind parietal peritoneum, front covered only, no mesentery, fixed.
Primary retro (never had mesentery)Secondary retro (mesentery fused)
Kidneys, ureters, suprarenals, aorta, IVCDuodenum D2–D4, pancreas except tail, ascending + descending colon

Exceptions: first 2 cm of duodenum and pancreatic tail (in splenorenal lig.) are intraperitoneal. Bladder = subperitoneal. Mnemonic SAD PUCKER: Suprarenal, Aorta/IVC, Duodenum, Pancreas, Ureters, Colon (asc./desc.), Kidneys, Esophagus, Rectum.

4Sacs, recesses, where fluid goes

  • Subphrenic recess: diaphragm ↔ top of liver, split R/L by falciform; abscess mostly right.
  • Hepatorenal (Morison) pouch: liver ↔ right kidney; greater sac; links lesser sac and right paracolic gutter → pelvis.

  • Sliding hiatal hernia: junction + cardia slide up. Paraesophageal: junction stays, fundus rolls up.

6Stomach

Epigastric + left hypochondrium. Cardia → fundus (dome above cardia, air bubble) → body → pyloric antrum → canal → pylorus. Lesser curvature has the angular incisure. Rugae inside; 3 muscle layers.

Stomach bed (behind, top → bottom): left diaphragm, spleen, left kidney + suprarenal, splenic a., pancreas, transverse mesocolon. Not bed: right kidney, appendix.

Pyloric stenosis (infant): thick pylorus → projectile, non-bilious vomiting, hungry baby, dehydration.

Arcades: lesser curvature = left + right gastric; greater curvature = left + right gastro-omental. Cut one → its partner fills the gap.

7Duodenum (D1) and GDA

  • C-loop around pancreatic head; first 2 cm intraperitoneal. D1 behind: GDA, bile duct, portal v., IVC. D1 in front: liver, gallbladder.
  • D2: major papilla (bile + pancreatic ducts). D3: SMA/SMV in front, IVC + aorta behind.
  • Above papilla = celiac (superior pancreaticoduodenal); below = SMA (inferior pancreaticoduodenal). They meet on the pancreatic head.

8Veins, lymph, nerves

  • L + R gastric vv. → portal v. directly. Short gastric + L gastro-omental → splenic v.; R gastro-omental → SMV. Prepyloric v. (marks pylorus) → R gastric.
  • Lymph follows arteries → celiac nodes → cisterna chyli (a sac, right of aorta, behind right crus) → thoracic duct.
  • Vagus: anterior trunk (left vagus), posterior trunk (right vagus); ↑ peristalsis, relaxes pylorus; no synapse in celiac plexus.
  • Sympathetic: greater splanchnic T5–T9 → celiac ganglion; ↓ peristalsis, closes pylorus.
  • Foregut pain → epigastric (pancreas → back). Vagal afferents carry fullness, not pain. Vagotomy ↓ acid.

3SMA

  • SMA order: inferior pancreaticoduodenal → middle colic (1st big right branch) → jejunal/ileal (fan to the left) → right colic → ileocolic (lowest; → appendicular a.).
  • SMA injection fills no IMA branches. DSA subtracts the pre-contrast image.
  • Superior (celiac via GDA) + inferior (SMA) pancreaticoduodenal aa. meet on the pancreatic head.
  • Marginal a. (Drummond): along the colon edge. Arc of Riolan: middle colic ↔ left colic, central. Weak spot: splenic flexure.
  • External iliac → common femoral → superficial femoral + profunda femoris.
